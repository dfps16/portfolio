Main project: BEng thesis, 2026–27

Onboard learning of comet 67P's gravity

When Rosetta reached comet 67P in 2014, its pre-arrival model was a smooth "rugby ball"; the real comet turned out to be two lobes joined by a neck, and ESA needed weeks of tracking to map its gravity before Philae could land. My thesis asks whether an onboard learner, handed that same wrong prior and replaying Rosetta's actual flown orbits, can correct its model of 67P's gravity, and whether it does so faster than a conventional estimator given the same data.

SupervisorProf. Alexander Wittig StatusIn progress

Method
A neural Hamiltonian ODE learner, following Acciarini & Izzo (2026), trained through the propagator. It is compared against a Kaula-regularised spherical-harmonic estimator on identical tracking arcs. The truth model is the geodesyNets 67P mascon model.
Success
Correcting the wrong prior at all; further success if it does so faster than the conventional estimator.

I led a team of five designing a lunar observation startup: a 24-satellite CubeSat constellation sized from what customers would pay for. We reached the finals.
Done
My role
Team lead of five. I owned the business case and set the mission requirements from the business needs: mission engineering.
Concept
A 24-satellite lunar CubeSat constellation in near-polar circular orbits at 250 km, spread across planes 18° apart in RAAN, giving nominal global coverage in about a day (1.2–1.5 days once lunar mascons perturb the ground track). VTVL descent simulator

A simulated rocket that lands upright on its own engine, used to compare two autopilots on how softly, accurately and fuel-efficiently they bring it down.
Done
Why I did it
Starworks, the team I lead, is building a vehicle that lands this way. I wanted to understand the control problem first-hand, and give the team a tested engine for its lander-trajectory tool.
Method
Planar 3-DOF nonlinear dynamics with real mass depletion, and separate touchdown and flameout events. A cascaded PD controller with dynamic inversion, taken through to a gain-scheduled LQR tuned with Bryson's rule. Scenarios are JSON files validated by Pydantic, with 14 regression tests.

What I learnt
Once mass depletes, the gentler LQR burns more propellant and flames out 2.9 m short, where the cascade lands with 2.1 kg to spare: its quadratic cost has no fuel term. Two of my tests also passed for the wrong reason, so I now check what a test can actually catch.

Relative navigation for a chaser spacecraft in LEO

A spacecraft trying to meet another in orbit has to work out where its target is from noisy distance and angle measurements. This project builds the filter that does it.
In progress
Why I did it
Navigation was the missing piece in my GNC work: I had guidance and control projects but no estimation. This is the estimation half of a rendezvous loop.
Method
Clohessy–Wiltshire dynamics, whose closed-form state-transition matrix makes propagation exact, so all the nonlinearity sits in the range and bearing measurements. An extended Kalman filter written from scratch, compared against dead reckoning and a least-squares fix. Tuned to statistical consistency (NEES and NIS inside chi-square bounds), then used to show that bearing-only tracking can't pin down range until the chaser manoeuvres.

What I learnt so far
Frame and sign conventions are where these projects go wrong, so I validated them on the bounded relative-motion ellipse before writing any filter. An error plot can't tell you whether a filter is well tuned; consistency tests can.
What's next
Feeding the estimate into a rendezvous controller, to close the full guidance, navigation and control loop.
