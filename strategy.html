Southampton University Spaceflight Society · Oct 2026

How we run SUSF's projects, 2026–27 onward

A one-page summary of the plan I wrote and presented as Head of Projects. SUSF grew as a set of projects, each started and led by whoever had the idea and the skills. This plan turns them into one society with shared goals.

Where we are going

This year · 26/27

  • Strong competition results for CubeSat and Mars Rover
  • An electric propulsion thruster test
  • A liquid bipropellant hot fire on our own test stand

Next one to two years · 27/28 – 28/29

  • Starworks full-stack lander test and hop
  • An application to ESA Fly Your Satellite
  • Further electric propulsion work

The problem

Strategy set project by project

Teams set their own direction, and collaboration between them relied on individual initiative.

Knowledge left with individuals

Capability sat with one or two people per team, and left when they graduated.

Outreach run team by team

Sponsor-seeking was fragmented across teams, and public posting was rare.

Four pillars

01

Knowledge

Intro to Space for every new member each autumn, a specialist electric propulsion curriculum, and a shared compendium, SharePoint and GitHub so knowledge stays with the society.

02

Direction

One society-wide strategy built around specific long-term goals, expressed through knowledge streams.

03

Governance

Clear expectations for team leads, defined review standards, regular communication between committee and leads, and funding conditional on agreed success criteria.

04

Profile and partnerships

One brand and one set of channels, with a society-level sponsor and funding pipeline run by new Media and Crowdfunding teams.

Knowledge streams

  • Propulsion
  • Vehicle
  • Avionics · GNC · FSW
  • Test · Qualification · Safety

Every project maps onto these streams. Together they show the capability we are building and where we are thin, which focuses recruitment and teaching, and lets us assess a new project proposal by the gap it fills.

This year's focus

  • Governance and strategy are the core aims.
  • Every project has clear success criteria, and its funding depends on them.
  • Every team reports its progress, or lack of it, regularly.
  • Stronger links between teams, through recurring socials and whole-society events.
